In three minutes, I will introduce SupportPortal Phase 1. This is not just a Zendesk replacement, and it is not another ticket UI. Phase 1 must prove that support quality checks, investigation collaboration, and management metrics can live inside the operating workflow instead of depending on Zendesk plus manual experience.

There are two reasons to do this now. First, Zendesk license renewal is about seventy-three thousand dollars a year, but it still gives us generic SaaS capability. Second, Zendesk does not give us enough customization space. Feature extension, internal workflow customization, data mining, and quality analytics are all hard to move at our own support cadence.

The Phase 1 migration strategy is conservative. The customer entry point stays unchanged, and the customer experience also stays unchanged. Customers still enter through Zendesk. Internally, Zendesk cases are forwarded into SupportPortal. We first prove routing, assignment, AI guardrail, final approval, dashboard, and real case replay through shadow mode and small-traffic gates.

Assignment is the Phase 1 dispatch control plane. The system is not only generating replies. It must decide who should handle the case, whether human review is needed, whether there is SLA risk, and how routing fallback should work. The assignment admin page is the manager control surface for shifts, rules, and manual override.

AgentRelay solves agent collaboration. Agents are not loosely chatting with each other. They exchange tasks, artifacts, and done criteria through AgentRelay. Why not pure A2A protocol? Because many personal agents do not have public IP addresses, so the relay handles forwarding, delivery, auditability, and fallback.

Support needs agent-to-agent communication because the Support Agent should not guess when it cannot solve a question. In this example, screen sharing stops around 01:28:44. The R&D Agent checks Argus events, finds a successful Client dot unpublish call, and concludes this was intentional application behavior, not an abnormal disconnection.

Now look at the engineer-side guardrail flow. The engineer writes a short draft: the camera is broken. The issue is not tone. It directly claims a root cause without traceable proof and without a safe customer next step.

AI Guardrail rejects an incomplete reply and tells the engineer that a root-cause claim needs traceable evidence, such as logs, reproduction result, device configuration difference, or an internal document reference.

After the engineer adds Web SDK log evidence, such as no input frame received, and gives a safe next step, such as asking the customer to retest with a different device, the system prepares a conservative customer draft and moves it toward approval.

Management metrics also need to evolve. We still track first response time, second response time, and SLA, but we also need AI guardrail pass rate, agent interaction count, first-contact resolution, reject reasons, and route accuracy. Phase 1 proves control. Phase 2 connects real evidence tools. Phase 3 expands governed autonomous investigation.
